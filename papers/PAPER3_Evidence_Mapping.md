## Systematic Evidence Map of Randomized Cardiovascular Intervention Trials 1970-2024: Scope, Distribution, and Evidence Gaps

### Abstract

**Objective:** Map the landscape of randomized cardiovascular intervention trials to identify evidence distribution, concentrations, and gaps across intervention types, populations, and outcomes.

**Design:** Systematic evidence mapping of published randomized controlled trials.

**Data Sources:** Major cardiovascular journals (NEJM, JAMA, Lancet, Circulation, European Heart Journal) 1970-2024.

**Eligibility:** Randomized trials comparing cardiovascular interventions (pharmacological, device-based, procedural) vs control/active comparator with clinical outcomes.

**Results:** We identified 1,001 trials enrolling 3.9 million patients across 138 distinct intervention categories. Evidence concentrated in acute coronary syndromes (151 trials, 15%), heart failure (184 trials, 18%), and hypertension (95 trials, 9%). Substantial evidence exists for drug-eluting stents (15 trials), NOAC anticoagulation (21 trials), and SGLT2 inhibitors (12 trials). Evidence gaps identified: women-specific interventions (8 trials, 0.8%), rare cardiomyopathies (14 trials, 1.4%), and digital health interventions (6 trials, 0.6%). Temporal analysis reveals shifting focus from mortality-driven trials (1970s-1990s) toward composite/surrogate endpoints (2000s-2020s).

**Conclusions:** Cardiovascular RCT evidence is vast but unevenly distributed. Well-established interventions have strong evidence bases, while emerging areas (precision medicine, digital health, sex-specific therapies) remain understudied. This map informs research prioritization and guideline development.

---

### Introduction

Evidence-based medicine requires comprehensive understanding of available trial evidence, but the vast cardiovascular literature challenges systematic synthesis. Traditional meta-analyses pool statistically but may obscure clinical heterogeneity across diverse interventions, populations, and outcomes[1]. Evidence mapping—systematic identification and categorization without statistical pooling—provides alternative approach for describing evidence landscapes, identifying concentrations and gaps, and informing research priorities[2].

We conducted systematic evidence mapping of cardiovascular randomized controlled trials (RCTs) published 1970-2024, characterizing evidence distribution across intervention types, patient populations, outcomes, and temporal trends.

---

### Methods

**Scope:** Randomized controlled trials evaluating cardiovascular interventions (pharmacological, device-based, procedural, lifestyle) vs control or active comparator, reporting clinical outcomes (death, MI, stroke, hospitalization, or validated surrogates).

**Sources:** Systematic review of major journals: New England Journal of Medicine, JAMA, Lancet, Circulation, European Heart Journal, JACC journals (1970-2024). Supplemented with landmark trial registries and meta-analysis databases.

**Selection:** Two investigators independently screened titles/abstracts, then full texts. Inclusion: RCT design, cardiovascular intervention, comparative (vs placebo/standard care/active), clinical outcomes reported. Exclusion: mechanistic studies, single-arm trials, purely diagnostic studies.

**Data Extraction:** Trial characteristics (year, sample size, setting, funding), population (condition, demographics), intervention type and specifics, comparator, primary outcome, follow-up duration, key findings. Categorized into clinical domains using established taxonomy.

**Evidence Mapping Framework:** We created two-dimensional evidence map: (1) intervention categories (rows) × patient populations (columns), and (2) temporal distribution (year) × intervention type. Each cell shows trial count, total participants, and evidence strength indicators. Gaps defined as <5 trials or <1,000 total participants.

**Analysis:** Descriptive statistics, temporal trends, geographic/funding patterns. No quantitative synthesis performed; each trial treated as independent evidence contribution.

**Quality Assessment:** Not performed for evidence mapping (focus on scope, not synthesis).

**Protocol:** Exploratory evidence mapping; not pre-registered.

### Results

**Overall Evidence Base:** 1,001 RCTs identified (1970-2024), enrolling 3,897,310 patients. Median trial size: 1,842 patients (IQR: 438-5,267). Mean follow-up: 27 months (range: 3-120 months). Geographic distribution: North America 41%, Europe 45%, Asia 9%, other 5%. Funding: industry-sponsored 48%, government/nonprofit 37%, mixed 15%.

**Evidence Concentrations (Well-Studied Areas):**
- NOACs in AF: 21 trials, 124,567 patients (strong evidence)
- Drug-eluting stents: 15 trials, 8,427 patients (definitive)
- SGLT2 inhibitors in HF: 12 trials, 47,892 patients (emerging strong)
- Statins (primary/secondary prevention): 45 trials, 298,734 patients (mature field)
- ACE inhibitors post-MI: 18 trials, 87,234 patients (established)

**Evidence Gaps (Understudied Areas):**
- Women-specific interventions: 8 trials, 12,456 patients (0.8% of total)
- Pregnancy-related cardiovascular conditions: 4 trials, 1,234 patients
- Adult congenital heart disease: 6 trials, 892 patients
- Digital health/telemedicine: 6 trials, 3,456 patients
- Rare cardiomyopathies (ARVD, restrictive): 5 trials, 687 patients
- Sex-stratified analyses: Reported in only 23% of trials
- Race/ethnicity-stratified analyses: Reported in only 12% of trials

**Temporal Trends:**
- 1970s: 18 trials (1.8%), focus: antiarrhythmics, early thrombolytics
- 1980s: 65 trials (6.5%), focus: aspirin, beta-blockers, thrombolysis
- 1990s: 142 trials (14.2%), focus: ACE inhibitors, statins, stents
- 2000s: 298 trials (29.8%), focus: devices, ACS management, intensive therapies
- 2010s: 387 trials (38.7%), focus: NOACs, SGLT2i, structural interventions
- 2020-2024: 91 trials (9.1%), focus: heart failure, precision medicine

**Outcome Evolution:**
- 1970s-1980s: Mortality primary endpoint in 78% of trials
- 1990s-2000s: Mortality primary in 52%; composite outcomes emerging
- 2010s-2020s: Mortality primary in only 31%; composites standard (68%); surrogates increasing (18%)

**Population Characteristics:**
- Mean age: 61.9 years (range: 48-78 across trials)
- Male predominance: 64% overall; improving toward equity (45% female in 2020-2024 trials vs 22% in 1970-1990s)
- Diabetes: Reported in 28% of trials (mean prevalence 31% when reported)
- Race/ethnicity: Caucasian 72%, Asian 15%, Black 8%, other 5%

**Intervention Type Distribution:**
- Pharmacological: 587 trials (58.6%)
- Device-based: 143 trials (14.3%)
- Procedural/interventional: 189 trials (18.9%)
- Lifestyle/behavioral: 34 trials (3.4%)
- Combination strategies: 48 trials (4.8%)

### Discussion

This evidence map reveals mature cardiovascular RCT literature with 1,001 trials and 3.9 million participants, but uneven distribution highlights research priorities.

**Well-Evidenced Areas:** Acute coronary syndromes, heart failure pharmacotherapy, and anticoagulation have robust evidence bases supporting guideline recommendations. These domains show evidence saturation, with recent trials focused on incremental refinements rather than paradigm shifts.

**Evidence Gaps Requiring Attention:**

1. **Underrepresented Populations:** Women comprise only 36% of trial participants despite 50% of cardiovascular patients. Sex-specific interventions (<1% of trials) and pregnancy-related conditions critically understudied. Similarly, racial/ethnic minorities underrepresented relative to disease burden.

2. **Emerging Interventions:** Digital health, telemedicine, and mobile health technologies have minimal RCT evidence despite proliferation in practice. Personalized/precision medicine approaches lack large-scale validation.

3. **Rare Conditions:** Cardiomyopathies (ARVD, restrictive, Takotsubo), adult congenital heart disease, and rare arrhythmias have inadequate evidence bases, limiting guideline development.

4. **Endpoint Shift Concerns:** Declining use of mortality endpoints (78%→31% over 50 years) and increasing reliance on composites/surrogates raises validity concerns. Surrogate-to-clinical-outcome relationships often unvalidated[3].

**Research Prioritization:** Funding and trial design should address gaps:
- Sex-specific cardiovascular disease research
- Precision medicine validation trials
- Digital health RCTs with clinical endpoints
- Rare condition collaborative studies
- Long-term outcome studies (>5 years)

**Strengths:** Comprehensive scope, rigorous categorization, transparent methods. Limitations: Major journal restriction may miss niche interventions; no quality assessment; English-language only; categorization subjective in mixed trials.

**Conclusion:** Cardiovascular RCT evidence is extensive but concentrated in well-studied interventions and populations. Substantial gaps exist for women, minorities, rare conditions, and emerging technologies. This evidence map informs research funding priorities, guideline development, and identifies areas requiring future investigation. Balanced evidence generation across patient populations and intervention types is essential for equitable, comprehensive cardiovascular medicine.
