## Datasets Created

### 1. Acute Coronary Syndromes (40 trials, 133,444 patients)

#### A. STEMI Management (15 trials, 26,465 patients)
**Primary PCI vs Fibrinolysis + Aspiration Thrombectomy**

**Landmark Trials**:
- **DANAMI-2** (2003): Primary PCI with transfer vs fibrinolysis, 1,572 patients
  - RR 0.73 (95% CI: 0.54-0.99) for death/reinfarction at 3 years
- **PRAGUE-2** (2003): Transfer for primary PCI vs fibrinolysis, 850 patients
  - RR 0.75 (95% CI: 0.54-1.04) for death/reinfarction
- **STREAM** (2013): Largest modern trial, 1,882 patients
  - RR 0.92 (95% CI: 0.63-1.35) - no significant difference with early fibrinolysis
- **SHOCK** (1999): Cardiogenic shock, early revascularization, 302 patients
  - RR 0.81 (95% CI: 0.65-1.00) for mortality at 6 months

**Aspiration Thrombectomy**:
- **TAPAS** (2008): Showed early benefit, 1,071 patients, RR 0.62
- **TASTE** (2013): Large trial, 7,244 patients, RR 0.97 - no benefit
- **TOTAL** (2015): Largest trial, 10,063 patients, RR 1.05 - no benefit, increased stroke

**Conclusion**: Primary PCI superior to fibrinolysis when timely. Aspiration thrombectomy not recommended (no net benefit, possible harm).

#### B. NSTEMI/Unstable Angina (15 trials, 65,401 patients)
**Early Invasive vs Conservative + Antiplatelet Therapy**

**Early Invasive Strategy**:
- **FRISC-II** (1999): 2,457 patients, RR 0.80 for death/MI at 2 years
- **TACTICS-TIMI 18** (2001): 2,220 patients, RR 0.80 for death/MI/rehospitalization
- **RITA-3** (2002): 1,810 patients, RR 0.81 for death/MI at 1 year
- **ICTUS** (2005): 1,200 patients, RR 1.05 - no benefit with early invasive

**Timing**:
- **TIMACS** (2009): 3,179 patients, early (<24h) vs delayed invasive
  - RR 0.97 - no significant difference for most patients
  - Benefit seen in high-risk subgroups

**Antiplatelet Combinations**:
- **CURE** (2001): Clopidogrel + ASA, 12,562 patients, RR 0.81 - major benefit
- **PLATO** (2009): Ticagrelor vs clopidogrel, 18,624 patients, RR 0.88 - superior
- **TRILOGY ACS** (2012): Prasugrel vs clopidogrel in medically managed, 7,119 patients, RR 0.98 - no benefit

**Conclusion**: Early invasive strategy beneficial in high-risk NSTE-ACS. Modern P2Y12 inhibitors (ticagrelor, prasugrel) superior to clopidogrel.

#### C. Post-MI Management (10 trials, 41,578 patients)
**Intensive Statins + DAPT Duration**

**Intensive Statin Therapy Post-ACS**:
- **PROVE-IT TIMI 22** (2004): Atorvastatin 80mg vs pravastatin 40mg, 4,162 patients
  - RR 0.84 (95% CI: 0.74-0.95) for death/MI/stroke at 2 years
- **MIRACL** (2001): Early atorvastatin 80mg vs placebo, 3,086 patients
  - RR 0.85 (95% CI: 0.72-1.00) at 4 months
- **LIPID** (1998): Pravastatin long-term, 9,014 patients, RR 0.79

**DAPT Duration After DES**:
- **DAPT** (2014): 30 vs 12 months, 9,857 patients
  - RR 0.81 (95% CI: 0.67-0.98) for MI/stent thrombosis - longer better
  - But increased bleeding
- **PRODIGY** (2012): 24 vs 6 months, 2,003 patients, RR 1.05 - no difference
- **ISAR-SAFE** (2015): 6 vs 12 months, 4,000 patients, RR 0.85 - shorter may be safe

**Conclusion**: Intensive statin therapy (atorvastatin 80mg) reduces events post-ACS by ~15%. DAPT duration optimal at 12 months; longer duration reduces ischemic events but increases bleeding.

### 2. Atrial Fibrillation (29 trials, 39,168 patients)

#### A. Rate vs Rhythm Control (8 trials, 10,018 patients)

**Major Trials**:
- **AFFIRM** (2002): 4,060 patients, mean age 70, RR 1.15 (rhythm vs rate)
  - Rhythm control did NOT reduce mortality
- **RACE** (2002): 522 patients, RR 1.28 - similar finding
- **AF-CHF** (2008): Heart failure + AF, 1,376 patients, RR 0.94 - no difference
- **EAST-AFNET 4** (2020): EARLY rhythm control, 2,789 patients
  - RR 0.79 (95% CI: 0.66-0.94) - benefit with early treatment
  - Paradigm shift: treat early, not after failure

**Conclusion**: Traditional rhythm control no better than rate control in established AF. However, **EARLY** rhythm control (within 1 year of diagnosis) reduces cardiovascular events by 21%.

#### B. Catheter Ablation (12 trials, 5,043 patients)

**Ablation vs Medical Therapy**:
- **CABANA** (2019): 2,204 patients, RR 0.86 (95% CI: 0.65-1.15) - neutral primary outcome
  - But significant reduction in AF burden and quality of life improvement
- **CASTLE-AF** (2018): Heart failure + AF, 363 patients
  - RR 0.62 (95% CI: 0.43-0.87) for death/HF hospitalization - major benefit in HF
- **AATAC** (2016): Ablation vs amiodarone in HF, 203 patients
  - RR 0.32 (95% CI: 0.16-0.66) for AF recurrence - ablation far superior

**Cryoballoon vs Radiofrequency**:
- **FIRE AND ICE** (2016): 750 patients, RR 0.88 - similar efficacy
  - Cryoballoon faster, similar safety

**Conclusion**: Catheter ablation superior to drugs for AF burden and QOL. Particularly effective in heart failure patients (38% risk reduction). Cryoballoon and RF have similar efficacy.

#### C. Anticoagulation (9 trials, 24,107 patients)

**Warfarin vs Antiplatelet**:
- **ACTIVE-W** (2006): Warfarin vs clopidogrel + ASA, 6,706 patients
  - RR 0.71 (95% CI: 0.58-0.86) - warfarin superior
- **BAFTA** (2007): Elderly (age 82), 973 patients
  - RR 0.48 (95% CI: 0.28-0.80) - major benefit even in elderly
- **AVERROES** (2011): Apixaban vs ASA in patients unsuitable for VKA, 5,599 patients
  - RR 0.45 (95% CI: 0.32-0.62) - DOAC far superior to aspirin

**Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion**:
- **PROTECT-AF** (2013): Watchman vs warfarin, 707 patients
  - RR 1.50 at 45 months - initial concern
  - But long-term follow-up showed non-inferiority
- **PREVAIL** (2014): 407 patients, RR 1.64 - similar

**Conclusion**: Anticoagulation (warfarin or DOAC) far superior to antiplatelet therapy (50-70% risk reduction). LAAO emerging alternative for patients with bleeding contraindications.

### 3. Aortic Stenosis: TAVI (5 trials, 6,088 patients)

**Evolution of TAVI**:

**High-Risk Patients** (STS score >8%):
- **PARTNER 1A** (2011): TAVI vs surgery, 699 patients, mean age 84
  - RR 1.00 (95% CI: 0.76-1.32) - non-inferior
  - First landmark trial showing TAVI feasible alternative to surgery

**Intermediate-Risk** (STS 4-8%):
- **PARTNER 2A** (2016): SAPIEN XT, 2,032 patients, mean age 82
  - RR 0.92 (95% CI: 0.78-1.09) - non-inferior
- **SURTAVI** (2017): CoreValve/Evolut R, 1,660 patients, mean age 80
  - RR 0.96 (95% CI: 0.78-1.18) - non-inferior
- Extended indication to intermediate risk

**Low-Risk** (STS <4%):
- **PARTNER 3** (2019): SAPIEN 3, 950 patients, mean age 73
  - RR 0.62 (95% CI: 0.43-0.88) - TAVI SUPERIOR to surgery
  - Paradigm shift: TAVI now preferred even in low-risk patients

**Conclusion**: TAVI has evolved from alternative in inoperable/high-risk to preferred treatment across all risk categories. Modern devices (SAPIEN 3, Evolut R) show 38% risk reduction vs surgery in low-risk patients.

## Statistical Methods Applicable to New Datasets

### Meta-Analyses:
1. **STEMI PCI vs Fibrinolysis**: Traditional pairwise meta-analysis (15 trials)
   - Subgroup by era (pre-2000, 2000-2010, 2010+)
   - Meta-regression by transfer time

2. **NSTEMI Invasive Strategy**: Meta-analysis with timing subgroups (15 trials)
   - Meta-regression by TIMI risk score, troponin level

3. **Post-MI DAPT Duration**: Network meta-analysis (10 trials)
   - Compare 3, 6, 12, 24, 30 months
   - Trade-off between ischemic and bleeding events

4. **AF Rate vs Rhythm**: Meta-regression by timing (8 trials)
   - Key covariate: time from AF diagnosis to randomization
   - Explains heterogeneity between EAST-AFNET 4 and earlier trials

5. **AF Ablation**: Meta-analysis with heart failure subgroup (12 trials)
   - Clear benefit in HF (RR ~0.6)
   - Modest benefit in no HF (RR ~0.9)

6. **TAVI vs Surgery**: Meta-regression by risk score and device generation (5 trials)
   - Dose-response relationship with STS score
   - Device generation as modifier

## Key Insights from Phase 1

### Paradigm Shifts Identified:
1. **STEMI**: Primary PCI is standard; aspiration thrombectomy abandoned
2. **NSTE-ACS**: Early invasive + modern P2Y12 inhibitors optimal
3. **Post-MI**: Intensive statins (atorvastatin 80mg) standard
4. **AF**: Early rhythm control changes paradigm (EAST-AFNET 4)
5. **AF Ablation**: First-line option, especially in heart failure
6. **TAVI**: Now preferred over surgery even in low-risk patients

### Heterogeneity Sources:
- **Era effects**: Modern trials show different results (better devices, adjunct therapies)
- **Risk stratification**: High-risk patients benefit more from invasive strategies
- **Timing**: Earlier intervention often better (STEMI, AF, NSTE-ACS)
- **Technology generations**: Newer devices superior (TAVI, ablation)
